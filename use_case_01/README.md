# Use case 1 — Take stock: what these four systems actually are

*AIGP Certification Training · Rathinam Trainers & Consultants*
**Module 1, Foundations** · sixty minutes in the room · delivers **Section A** of the
Kabini AI Governance Pack

Kabini Workforce Services runs four AI systems and has no inventory of them. This hour
writes the first one: a register with a row for MatchScore, Nora, the right-to-work check
and Cadence, each classified as classic, generative or agentic, each described as a
*system* rather than a model, and each saying whether the model behind it is used as-is,
fine-tuned, retrieval-augmented or agentic.

**Nothing here runs.** There is no program, no install, no account and no network. The
deliverable is Markdown, and that is the point — this is the kind of document somebody
signs.

## How the hour runs

The concept pages come first and in order — model versus system, the three types and the
order to ask them in, the realisation axis. Then the register: the trainer builds the
MatchScore row on screen and gets it wrong on purpose, writing it as a description of a
*model* before repairing it field by field into a description of a *system*. The room
fills the other three rows from the worksheet in `demo/A_annex_3_room_worksheet.md`, and
the close is the finished register read back with its open items.

## Where this stops

The register says what each system **is**, and no further. It names no harm, assigns
nobody the developer, provider, deployer or user label, states no risk tier and quotes no
legal obligation or date — those are use cases 2, 3, 26 and later. Page 06 of the concepts
records that personal data sits outside the Union and deliberately draws no conclusion
from it.
